## Things Claude noticed (Supported inference unless a card is named; not conclusions)

- **$c$ plays two roles.**
  - *Inside one theory*, $c$ is invariant: every observer measures the same $c$, and it sits inside the form, $x^2 - c^2t^2$ (S5c).
  - *Across theories*, $c$ is a parameter: as it varies, its predictions $Y(c)$ trace a path on the model manifold, and $c \to \infty$ is that path's limit (S5d).

  Your phrase "an invariant variable that acts as a rule in the manifold" could refer to either role, or to the passage from one to the other (see open question 1).
- **An unbounded parameter can reach a bounded geometry in two different ways.**
  - *Wrapping:* $t \mapsto (\cos \omega t, \sin \omega t)$. There is no limit as $t \to \infty$; the point returns forever.
  - *Compressing:* $s \mapsto \tanh s$ (S5b), and $c \mapsto Y(c)$ (S5d). Here there **is** a limit, at the edge of the bounded set.

  Your two kinds of infinity (answer 6) both sit inside the wrapping case. $c \to \infty$ is a compressing case. This is proposal [C8](../connections/proposed.md).
