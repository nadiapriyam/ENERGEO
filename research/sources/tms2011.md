# Source card: Transtrum, Machta, Sethna 2010 and 2011 (width definitions)

- **M. K. Transtrum, B. B. Machta, J. P. Sethna.** "Geometry of nonlinear least squares with applications to sloppy models and optimization." *Physical Review E* 83, 036701 (2011). DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.83.036701; arXiv [1010.1449](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1449).
- **M. K. Transtrum, B. B. Machta, J. P. Sethna.** "Why are nonlinear fits to data so challenging?" *Physical Review Letters* 104, 060201 (2010). arXiv [0909.3884](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3884).
- **Read on 2026-11-15:** the full arXiv texts. The published journal versions were not read.

## Exact quotations (arXiv 1010.1449, §III)

**How the widths are computed:**

> The widths in Fig. 6 were calculated by considering geodesic motion in each of the eigendirections of the metric from some point located near the center of the model manifold. We follow the geodesic motion until it reaches a boundary; the length in data space of the geodesic is the width.

**An alternative definition:**

> Alternatively, we can choose M − N orthogonal unit vectors that span the space perpendicular to the tangent plane at a point and a single unit vector given by a eigenprediction of the Jacobian which lies within the tangent plane. The M − N + 1 dimensional hyper-plane spanned by these unit vectors intersects the model manifold along a one-dimensional curve. The width can be taken to be the length of that intersection. The widths given by these two methods are comparable.

**Units and parameterization:**

> Strictly speaking, the singular values of the Jacobian have units of data space distance per unit parameter space distance, while the units of the widths are data space distance independent of parameters. ... In general, the exact agreement between the singular values and the widths may not agree if the parameters utilize different units or have another natural scale.

**Parameter choice in the exponential example:**

> We use log-parameters ... to make parameters dimensionless and enforce positivity.

**Fig. 6 caption:**

> Geodesic cross-sectional widths of an eight dimensional model manifold along the eigendirections of the metric from some central point, together with the square root of the eigenvalues (singular values of the Jacobian)

**The 2010 letter** (arXiv 0909.3884, Fig. 3 caption) says the same: "Geodesic cross-sectional widths of an eight dimensional model manifold along the eigendirections of the metric".

## Consequence for the project

- **Each geodesic length is coordinate invariant.** It is a data-space length.
- **The collection of widths is not defined invariantly.** Two choices enter it:
  - the starting point, "some point located near the center", which is not specified;
  - the initial directions, which are the eigendirections of the metric in the chosen parameter coordinates (log-parameters here). These directions rotate with the chart (MMPg: 0.626° for log θ and 4.43° for a change of units at one F-C point).
  - The alternative method uses an "eigenprediction of the Jacobian", which is chart-dependent in the same way.
- **The authors themselves** say the agreement with singular values depends on units ("may not agree if the parameters utilize different units").
- **Decision (C7 pass 1, 2026-11-15):** Transtrum-style geodesic widths are not used as C7's primary invariant object. The C7 target is the Quinn-style enclosure (`research/sources/quinn2019.md`).
