## Your clarifications

From [Gate 2](../../docs/gate2_answers.md):

- **Wording (answer 6).** "An unbounded time parameter combined with a finite circular geometry generates an indefinitely continuing sine/cosine evolution whose values remain bounded."
  - There are two infinities: $t \to \infty$, and the number of completed cycles $\to \infty$.
  - Throughout, $\sin \omega t, \cos \omega t \in [-1, 1]$.
  - **The conclusion is not programmed into any interactive.**
- **The circle example (answer 8).** The unbounded object is $t \in \mathbb R$, the bounded geometry is $S^1$, and combining is $t \mapsto (\cos\omega t, \sin\omega t)$. The question: does an unbounded quantity expressed through a bounded geometry have deeper analogues elsewhere?
- **$c \to \infty$ (answer 3)** is a parameter limit in a family of theories, $c \mapsto Y(c)$, not a physical process. **"The manifold" (answer 4)** is the prediction/model manifold $\mathcal Y = \{Y(\theta)\}$.

From [Gate 3](../../docs/gate3_decisions.md):

- **Which role of $c$ is "the rule" (H3-1).** Primarily *the relationship between the two roles*:
  - inside a fixed theory, $c$ is an invariant constant that helps define the transformation geometry and the interval;
  - across theories, $c$ is a parameter, and $c \to \infty$ moves toward the Newtonian boundary.

  The question is whether a meaningful pattern exists in which a parameter defines the invariant structure within each theory, while varying that parameter moves us through a family of such structures. *Not* to be stated as proving that $c$ is "the rule of the manifold".
- **Breadth (H3-2).** The common object of investigation is "an unbounded parameter interacting with or being represented through a constrained geometry". Wrapping, quasi-periodic winding and boundary approach are compared, and are **not** assumed to be the same structure. C8's split is "useful evidence, not a problem". The eventual theory may be a classification of several related geometries.
