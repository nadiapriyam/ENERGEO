## Your clarifications

From `docs/claude_code_prompt.md` §1b:

- It's not a pattern *between* $\hat f(k)$ and $e^{2\pi i k t}$. What these terms represent *together* is seen everywhere in Fourier series and the Fourier transform.
- A Fourier coefficient is a projection: $\langle f, e^{2\pi i n t}\rangle = \int_0^1 f(t)\, e^{-2\pi i n t}\, dt = \hat f(n)$, so $f(t) = \sum_k \langle f, e^{2\pi i k t}\rangle\, e^{2\pi i k t}$.
- *Premise, not a question for Claude:* "how to get an orthonormal basis when you can't visualize it." Gate 2 answer 2 narrows "infinite things converge": convergence belongs to a limiting process such as $S_N \to f$.

From [Gate 2](../../docs/gate2_answers.md), answer 7: "beyond Euclidean" means other measuring rules ($q_M$ with $M \ne I$) and spaces that can't be seen directly. We *access* those geometries through representations. The visuals should reveal relationships, especially orthogonality, that exist even when we can't literally see them.

From [Gate 3](../../docs/gate3_decisions.md):

- **The significance of orthogonality (H1-1).** Orthogonality makes components mathematically separable. If $x = \sum_k a_k e_k$ with orthonormal $e_k$, then $\langle e_i, e_j\rangle = 0$ for $i \ne j$ removes the cross terms, and $a_k = \langle x, e_k\rangle$ isolates one component independently of the others. The testable signature is:
  1. zero cross terms;
  2. independent projection coefficients;
  3. Pythagorean/Parseval additivity;
  4. the projection residual is perpendicular to the retained component.

  Where the space can't be drawn, the Gram matrix $G_{ij} = \langle e_i, e_j\rangle$ makes the geometry visible: $G = I$ for an orthonormal basis. Perturbing the basis away from orthogonality makes off-diagonal entries appear, brings the cross terms back, and breaks the clean decomposition. **That contrast is the demonstration.**
- **Which zero (H1-2).** The primary zero is $\langle u, v\rangle_M = 0$, orthogonality. The null vector, $q_M(v) = 0$ with $v \ne 0$, is a separate related phenomenon, possible in indefinite geometries. **Do not merge the two.** H1 establishes orthogonality first and compares it with null geometry later.
