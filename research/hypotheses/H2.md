## Your clarifications

From [Gate 2](../../docs/gate2_answers.md), answer 1: "develop" means how one mathematical representation leads into or reveals another representation **of the same underlying object**: "transform, extend, or reinterpret an object through successive mathematical representations while tracking what remains invariant." Answer 9 says the Master Map's "journey of one signal" is the interactive sequence for this.

From [Gate 3](../../docs/gate3_decisions.md):

- **The candidate pattern (H2-1).**
  $$\text{Object} \rightarrow \text{Representation} \rightarrow \text{Transformation} \rightarrow \text{Invariant / preserved relationship} \rightarrow \text{Decomposition or coordinates} \rightarrow \text{Geometry}$$
  Attached to it is the question: *what became visible, and what information became hidden or lost?*
  - A representation is valuable because it can make a relationship simple that was hard to see in the previous one. Example: translation is a shift in the signal, and multiplication by a phase on each Fourier mode.
  - "I am not claiming every mathematical representation must follow it. That is what needs testing."
- **"Due to this geometry" (H2-2).** Both, in different roles:
  - $M$, or the inner product, **defines** the geometry (magnitude, orthogonality).
  - Orthogonality **organizes** independent directions and components inside that geometry.
  - An invariant form $A^\dagger M A = M$ says which relationships survive a transformation.

  These are not the same concept.
- **What false looks like (H2-3).** No consistent structural relationship among representation, transformation, invariant and geometry. Concretely, any of these:
  - an important representation change with no meaningful preserved relationship;
  - an example where the decomposition mechanism doesn't apply;
  - incompatible meanings of "invariant" forced together;
  - unrelated architectures needed for most examples.

  Keep every failure. The outcome may be several families of representation geometry rather than one pattern.
