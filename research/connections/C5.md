# C5. "The quadratic equaling 0" has two faces

*Supporting connection (kept at Gate 3).*

## Statement (§4)

"Equaling 0" shows up in two ways:

- orthogonality, $\langle u,v\rangle_M = 0$;
- null vectors, $Q(v) = 0$.

In Minkowski geometry, light is orthogonal to itself. Mass is the invariant form of 4-momentum: $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$ is Block 9's interval in other variables.

## Where it might break (Claude's notes)

- **Positive-definite geometries.** If $M$ is positive-definite ($I$, a weighted $M$, Fisher $g$), then $Q(v) = 0$ forces $v = 0$. The two faces only come apart in indefinite geometries such as $\eta$. So C5 is a statement *about* indefinite forms, not about all of them.

## What an interactive would show

- **Block 1:** the saddle $z = x^2 - y^2$, with its null lines at height 0. Drag a vector onto a null line and its M-orthogonal direction turns until it lies on top of it.
- **Block 10:** the mass shell $\omega^2 = c^2k^2 + m^2c^4/\hbar^2$ as a hyperboloid that closes into the light cone as $m \to 0$. It is the same surface as Block 9's.

## What would move it up a level

Already Level 1. Settled at Gate 3 (H1-2): **the primary zero in H1 is orthogonality**, $\langle u,v\rangle_M = 0$. The null vector, $q_M(v) = 0$ with $v \ne 0$, is a separate phenomenon, compared later. The two are never merged.
