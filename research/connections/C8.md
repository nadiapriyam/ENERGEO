# C8. Unbounded parameters can generate different kinds of constrained asymptotic geometry

*Approved at Gate 3 and reworded. Claude proposed it, prompted by your Gate 2 answer 8.*

## Statement

An unbounded parameter interacting with, or represented through, a constrained geometry does **not** produce only one asymptotic behavior. At least three cases are distinguished, and each keeps its own mathematical setting:

| Case | Example | Behavior at infinity | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wrapping / periodic** | $t \mapsto (\cos\omega t, \sin\omega t)$ | The domain is unbounded and the target is $S^1$. There is no limit, and the point returns exactly, forever. Your two infinities ($t$ and cycle count) live here. | C8a PASS |
| **Quasi-periodic winding** | the shift orbit of $\cos t + \cos\sqrt2\,t$ winds through a torus | No limit and no exact return, but recurrence arbitrarily close: returns at $\tau = 2\pi q$ for continued-fraction denominators $q$ | C8d PASS; B5c PASS (rational $r$ gives exact returns) |
| **Boundary approach / convergence** | $\tanh s$; $\theta \mapsto e^{-\theta t_i}$; Fourier partial sums $S_N$; the theory-space limit $c \to \infty$ | A limiting state or boundary is approached, under a suitable representation or parameterization | S5b, C8b PASS; C8c **FAIL** then C8c2 PASS; S5d PASS |

**The boundary-approach examples live in different settings and are kept separate:**

- $\tanh s$ is a bounded function with a limit at the edge of its range.
- $S_N \to f$ converges in mean square, $\|S_N - f\|_2 \sim N^{-1/2}$, but not pointwise: the overshoot at a jump never shrinks. C8c assumed the error falls like $N^{-1}$ (that is the *squared* error) and FAILED. **That FAIL stays permanently.** C8c2 was added after it, with the theory's own prediction as its criterion.
- $c \to \infty$ is a limit in **theory space**: the classical theory occurs as a boundary or limit of the model manifold ([source](../sources/quinn2023.md)). **$Y(c)$ is not claimed to lie in a bounded set, and the model manifold is not claimed to be bounded.**

## Where it breaks

The cases behave *oppositely* at infinity: exact recurrence, near recurrence, and convergence. Your Gate 3 reading: "useful evidence, not a problem". The eventual theory may be a classification of several related geometries. A new example that fits none of the three is added as a new row, not forced into one.
