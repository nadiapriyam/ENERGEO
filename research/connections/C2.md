# C2. Conservation = invariant form, infinitesimally

*Supporting connection (kept at Gate 3, with its limits).*

## Statement (§4)

For **symmetric** $M$, a linear flow $z' = Kz$ conserves $z^T M z$ exactly when $K^T M + M K = 0$. Symmetry → conservation → constraint → allowed motion is this one condition.

## Where it breaks

- **Antisymmetric $M$ (carded C2a).** $z^TJz \equiv 0$, so *every* flow "conserves" it. The equivalence fails: $K = I$ conserves $z^TJz$, yet $K^TJ + JK = 2J$. Symplectic preservation is the condition $K^TJ + JK = 0$ itself, not the conservation of a quadratic ([C1](C1.md)).

- **Nonlinear flows.** A pendulum conserves $H = p^2/2 - \cos q$, which is not of the form $z^T M z$. There the condition becomes Noether's, not $K^T M + M K = 0$.
- **Dissipative flows.** When $\operatorname{tr} K \ne 0$ (damping), no nonzero quadratic form is conserved. The motion spirals in or out, with no level set to stay on.

Quadratic invariance, Noether conservation, symplectic preservation and metric invariance are connected here but **not interchangeable** (Gate 3 process rule).
