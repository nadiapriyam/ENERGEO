# C6. Projection by symmetry averaging is orthogonal projection

*Supporting connection (kept at Gate 3).*

## Statement (§4)

$P_H^2 = P_H$, $P_H^T = P_H$, and the residual is perpendicular to the symmetric part. Representation theory decomposes by projecting.

## Level

1 for finite groups acting by orthogonal or unitary maps.

## Where it might break (Claude's notes, not carded)

- **Non-unitary actions.** Averaging still gives a projection, but not an *orthogonal* one, unless the inner product is averaged first (Weyl's unitary trick).
- **Non-compact groups.** There is no normalized average over all real translations; a finite sum or a normalized Haar integral doesn't exist. Projecting onto translation-invariant pieces then becomes the Fourier transform with a *continuous* spectrum. This is the compact / non-compact split again (Peter–Weyl vs Pontryagin, both uncarded and listed in §5), and it ties C6 to [C3](C3.md).

## What an interactive would show

Block 7:

- A three-sample signal is a point in $\mathbb R^3$.
- Cyclic shifting is a 120° rotation about $(1,1,1)$, so the orbit is an equilateral triangle.
- $P_H x$ is the triangle's centroid on the diagonal, and the residual lies in the plane perpendicular to it.

## What would move it up a level

A card for the non-compact case: averaging over growing windows $[-T, T]$ of translations converges to the zero-frequency component.
