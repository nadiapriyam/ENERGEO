# C7. Sloppiness resembles spectral decay (open analogy)

*Level 4 / open analogy (Gate 3). **Not evidence.***

## Statement

Fisher eigenvalues of a sum of exponentials are roughly evenly spaced on a log scale, like decaying Fourier coefficients. "Infinitely many parameters combined into a finite effective geometry" (the hyperribbon) may resemble the pattern of Bessel's inequality.

## Level

**4. Open or speculative connection.** It stays here until a mathematically precise mapping exists (Gate 3).

## Why it is not yet a connection

These are different objects (your Gate 3 wording):

- **Bessel** is about the coefficients of *one vector or function* in an orthonormal family: $\sum_k |\langle f, e_k\rangle|^2 \le \|f\|^2$.
- **The sloppy spectrum** is the eigenvalues of a Fisher or sensitivity *metric*.

One possible pairing: Fisher eigenvectors play $e_k$, and a prediction displacement or tangent vector plays $f$. Then Bessel/Parseval describes the coordinates of that displacement. But **that does not by itself explain why the Fisher eigenvalues decay.**

**Rule:** Bessel's inequality is not used as evidence for sloppiness until an actual relationship is derived. S14a stays on the ledger.

## What would move it up a level

1. **A derivation:** a stated relationship between the eigenvalue decay of $J^TJ$ and the coefficient decay of some specified $f$ in a specified basis, with its own card.
2. **A test of the spacing claim fixed in advance:** the number of parameters, the parameter draws, the measurement times and the spacing criterion, all stated *before* computing, then run over many random draws.

S14a and S14b stay on the ledger whatever happens.
