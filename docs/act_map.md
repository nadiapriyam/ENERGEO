**Placement of the remaining Foundation items (decided 2026-11-12).**

- **Part / whole** closes Act III, with the governing question: *What is the visible object/result to the larger
  geometry that generated it?* This is the mathematically grounded precursor to "What are we to the fractal?". The
  R4 construction is not a fractal; it only supplies a precise part/whole representation example.
- **Representation** is Act III's core structure: larger object → representation / map → visible manifestation.
  Every Act III construction records the representation-change template (`research_method.md` §3):
  - source object, map, target object;
  - what survives, what collapses, what becomes zero;
  - what remains reconstructible (map known / unknown);
  - what information is lost.
- **Invariant relation** has no Act of its own. It is the recurring question asked after every representation
  change, in every Act: *What survived?* and *What disappeared or collapsed?* The invariant is never assumed known in
  advance.

## Stage 4 contract · A Shape From One Rule (Act II opening, not built)

The stage bridges *zero relation → orthogonal relation → constraint → shape*. It follows DR-1 to DR-4.

**Object.** A center O, a fixed radius r, a moving endpoint x(θ), and the trace of allowed endpoints.

**Manipulation.** The learner chooses a radius, which then stays fixed, and rotates the compass arm.

**Measurement (left pane), only what is needed:**

1. the distance ‖x(θ) − O‖;
2. the arm's direction (the angle turned so far);
3. later, the relation between the arm and the trace's direction of motion at the endpoint.

**Discovery order:**

| Phase | What happens | What the learner sees |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | Blank plane, one point O, an arm to one movable endpoint. No shape drawn. | distance; direction |
| Manipulate | Choose r (then fixed); rotate the arm; the endpoint leaves a trace. | the trace growing |
| Detect | The trace closes on itself. The observation record lists what stayed fixed (O, r) and what changed (direction, endpoint). Rotating further adds nothing new to the trace while the angle turned keeps growing. | record: center unchanged, distance unchanged, direction changing, endpoint changing, boundary formed; angle turned keeps increasing |
| Conjecture (action) | Predict the reading of the arm–motion angle at a point not yet probed. | the learner's prediction, with no badge |
| Test | Probe the tangent (local direction of motion) at several learner-chosen points. | the angle between arm and tangent, read each time (90°) |
| Formalize | x(θ) = O + r(cos θ, sin θ); (x(θ) − O) · x′(θ) = 0. With the precise statement (G1d): fixed distance from O ↔ position (relative to O) orthogonal to velocity, for a differentiable curve on a connected parameter interval. | the formal rule, for the first time |

**Hidden until formalize:** the words *circle*, *orthogonal*, *perpendicular*, *sin*, *cos*, *π* and *periodic* as
explanatory terms. The degree reading "90°" is a measurement, not an explanation.

**Never written:** "orthogonality caused the circle", "orthogonality creates sine and cosine".

**Ends on (research questions, no answers):**
- Which fixed relationships made the shape possible?
- Which global rules are consequences of those fixed relationships?

These feed B-ORTHO-FORM, B-FINITE-INFINITE and T-FIXED-RELATIONS-Q.

**Proof obligations it opens** (B-ORTHO-FORM's construction, not learner copy):
- What geometric constraints produce the circular parameterization?
- Which quantities depend on the fixed-radius rule?
- Which depend specifically on Euclidean metric structure?
- Where does π enter?
- Where do sine/cosine enter?
- Which of these relations generalize to other geometries?
