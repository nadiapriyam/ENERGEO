# C1. One equation, many geometries

*Claude's synthesis: to be tested, not settled.*

## Statement (`claude_code_prompt.md` §4)

$A^T M A = M$ holds in four settings:

- $M = I$: rotation, and Parseval as a unitary map.
- $M = \eta$: Lorentz transformations.
- $M = J$: symplectic maps, i.e. Hamiltonian flow.
- $M = g(\theta)$: the Fisher metric, which varies from point to point.

My "invariant that acts as a rule in the manifold" may be $M$ itself.

## Where it might break (Claude's notes, not carded)

- **When $M$ varies from point to point.** With $M = g(\theta)$, $A^T g(\theta) A = g(\theta)$ still makes sense *at one point*: it picks out the linear maps of that tangent space that preserve the metric there. But a curved model manifold generally has **no** transformations of the whole manifold that preserve $g$ everywhere (no isometries). So the equation survives pointwise while the group may not survive globally.
- **When $c$ is a parameter.** Spacetime's form contains $c$. Along $c \to \infty$, $M$ itself changes and degenerates (proposal [C9](proposed.md), carded C9a–d).
