# C3. The sign of the invariant decides bounded vs unbounded

*Claude's synthesis: to be tested, not settled.*

## Statement (§4)

These are the same trichotomy:

- the sign of $J^2$, which makes the motion a rotation, a boost or a shear;
- the sign of the energy in Kepler orbits: ellipse, parabola or hyperbola.

"Infinite time + finite circle → bounded sine and cosine" is the compact case.

## Level

| Part | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Each trichotomy on its own | 1 | Carded. |
| That they are *the same* trichotomy | Supported inference | No card links them yet. |

**Candidate card to link them (a known result, uncarded).** The Kepler problem's conserved quantities (angular momentum $L$ and the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector) generate a symmetry algebra that depends on the energy (Fock 1935, Bargmann 1936):

| Energy | Algebra | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| $E < 0$ | $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ | compact |
| $E = 0$ | $\mathfrak{e}(3)$ | contraction (boundary case) |
| $E > 0$ | $\mathfrak{so}(3,1)$ | non-compact |

That is the same compact / boundary / non-compact split as S4, at the level of groups. A Poisson-bracket card would make "same" precise.

## Where it might break (Claude's notes)

- **Flows with trace.** If $\operatorname{tr} J \ne 0$, no form is preserved and orbits spiral. The trichotomy doesn't apply.
- **Higher dimensions.** Forms with mixed signature, such as $(p, q)$, have more than three cases.
- **Middle cases look alike but may not be.** Shear is not a "small rotation" or a "small boost". It sits on the boundary between them, just as the parabola does. Whether these two boundaries are the same thing is part of what the card above would test.
- **$c \to \infty$ lands on the boundary.** The boost generator crosses from hyperbolic to shear in that limit (carded C9c, proposal [C9](proposed.md)).

## What an interactive would show

One slider moves through $\det J > 0$, then $0$, then $< 0$. The orbit goes ellipse → straight line → hyperbola. Beside it, a Kepler orbit with an energy slider crosses the same boundary at $E = 0$, with both readouts live.
