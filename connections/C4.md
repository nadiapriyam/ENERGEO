# C4. The Fourier transform is itself a rotation

*Claude's synthesis: to be tested, not settled.*

## Statement (§4)

The Fourier transform is a quarter-turn in time–frequency phase space: the harmonic-oscillator flow run for a quarter period. Its eigenvectors are the Hermite functions, with eigenvalues $(-i)^n$, and $F^4 = I$. So the representation change of Block 3 is a group action of Block 1, with eigenvalues.

## Level

1 for the Fourier transform on $L^2(\mathbb R)$. Turning by any angle gives the fractional Fourier transform, which is known but uncarded.

## Where it might break (Claude's notes, not carded)

- **Fourier series vs Fourier transform.** Your evidence statement uses Fourier *series*, $\hat f(k)\, e^{2\pi i k t}$, on the circle. That map takes functions on $S^1$ to sequences on $\mathbb Z$, which is a *different* space. It is unitary (Parseval), but "$F^4 = I$" and "rotation of one phase space" don't apply as stated. C4 holds for the transform on $\mathbb R$, and it's open how it carries over to the circle.

## What an interactive would show

The $(x, k)$ phase-space plane in 3D, with a wave packet's Wigner function as height. Its shadows on the two walls are $|\psi(x)|^2$ and $|\hat\psi(k)|^2$.

- An angle slider turns the packet. At $\alpha = \pi/2$ the shadows swap: that is $F$.
- At $\alpha = 2\pi$ the packet is back where it started: $F^4 = I$.
